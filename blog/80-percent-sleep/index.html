80% sleep?

2026-10-25

recently i've been experimenting with sleep. or, more accurately, with the amount of time i spend arguing with my alarm.

i used to sleep 9–10 hours a day. i'd set an alarm for 8 am because i've always wanted to be one of those people who wake up early, get their work done, and have the rest of the day to themselves. then i'd snooze it, wake up at 11, and spend the first few minutes cursing myself. great start to the day.

then i came across an interesting way of thinking about sleep: what if you treated it like a recovery percentage?

say 8–9 hours gives you 100% recovery. what if 6–7 hours gives you 80%, but also gives you an extra hour or two to work? wouldn't 80% of yourself, with more time, sometimes be a better deal than 100% with less?

and sure, you might feel a little more tired. but hey, we've got caffeine ;)

the more i think about it, though, the more i realize the math isn't that simple. those percentages are just a thought experiment, not actual measurements. two extra hours awake don't automatically mean two extra hours of useful work. if i'm tired, distracted, or rereading the same line of code five times, what did i really gain?

also, caffeine can make you feel more alert. it can't magically replace the sleep you skipped. and for most adults, the CDC recommends at least seven hours.

so maybe the experiment isn't about forcing myself to get by on less sleep. maybe it's about making the hours i already have count: going to bed earlier, actually getting up when the alarm rings, and paying attention to how well i work instead of just how long i'm awake.

i still find the 80% idea interesting. i just don't want to optimize for hours on the clock and accidentally make myself worse at using them.

more hours ≠ more progress. annoying, but probably true.
