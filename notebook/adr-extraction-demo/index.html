Reading a post for reported drug reactions

A step-by-step sketch of the general workflow my undergraduate research studies: finding drug and reaction mentions in informal text, pairing them, and checking each pair against what the writer actually said.

Illustration only: the posts and drug names are invented, and no real data, models, or results are used.

Step through an example

  1. 1. Original text

    The input is informal writing. People use brand names, slang, typos, and hedges, and they describe other people’s experiences as well as their own.

  2. 2. Drug mentions

    The first pass marks spans that name a medication. In the real setting this means handling spelling variants and mapping names to a standard vocabulary.

  3. 3. Reaction mentions

    The next pass marks spans that describe a symptom or side effect. Casual phrases are mapped to a standard term, shown in small type, so “I can’t sleep” and “insomnia” can be compared.

  4. 4. Candidate pairs

    Drug and reaction mentions in the same post are joined into candidate pairs. A candidate is only a hypothesis to check; at this point nothing has been decided.

  5. 5. Review against the text

    Each candidate is checked against the words that support or contradict it. Pairs can be supported, contradicted, flagged for a human decision, or left out when the evidence is too weak. The research plan compares direct prompting, retrieval-augmented generation, and extraction with verification against human annotations.

  6. 6. A report is not proof of causation

    Even a well-supported pair only says that someone reported a reaction while taking a drug. It does not show the drug caused it: the timing could be coincidence, other medications or conditions could be involved, and online posts are not a representative sample. Extracted pairs are signals for further study, not medical findings, and nothing on this page is medical advice.

Key

  • Drug a medication mention
  • Reaction a symptom or side-effect mention
  • Supported the text backs the pair
  • Contradicted the text rules the pair out
  • Flagged needs a human decision
  • Abstain evidence too weak to assert
